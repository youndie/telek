## Goal

The engine is done. Per-chat actors with ordering, async effects through `Event`, opt-in debounce,
`:testing`, three targets, README samples that are compiled by `:docs-samples` — 0.1.0 through 0.3.0
went into correctness and infrastructure, and polishing further has little left to return.

What remains is not in the engine. It is in three things: an **input model** that has to be settled
before any version becomes permanent, a **release** that lets a stranger depend on this at all, and a
**real bot** that reorders the remaining debts by what actually hurts rather than by how they look
from inside the library.

## Decisions that shaped the order

**Central comes last, not first.** The obvious ordering puts distribution first: it is cheap, and it
blocks everything downstream. It is still wrong here. A version on Maven Central is permanent — it
cannot be rewritten or withdrawn — so publishing before [B-01](docs/backlog/B-01-conversation-key.md)
makes the first version a stranger can resolve also the one the next release breaks. A Reposilite
snapshot costs nothing to get wrong. Central does not. Hence `stage-0` before `stage-1`.

**The key and the address are two things that happen to coincide.** `Telek.onInput(chatId, input)`
takes the key as a parameter, and nothing in `:core` ever reads `input.chatId` — so the `chatId` on
`Input` and on the effects is the Telegram *address*, not the key. In a private chat they are equal;
in a group they are not. That is what makes B-01 tractable rather than a rewrite.

**The debts are ordered by guess, and the guess is labelled.** `stage-3` is ordered by how the gaps
feel from inside the library. [B-07](docs/backlog/B-07-real-bot-in-production.md) exists to replace
that ordering with evidence, and an item it contradicts should be reordered, not defended.
